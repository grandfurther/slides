EXCLUSIVE

Owner buys $40K of software. Customers still can’t tell what they sell.

An investigation into wasted marketing budgets finds the same culprit again and again: a fuzzy offer on the first screen, long before any tool was bought.

By Chris Basil, Founder & EditorVelquiry Newsroom4 min read

The demo was flawless. The dashboard glowed green. Eleven months and one annual contract later, the phone at a family-run contracting firm rang no more often than it had before.

It is a story this paper hears every week. A business owner senses something is off with marketing, and the market answers with a product: a new CRM, an AI chatbot, an automation suite, a retainer with a sharp-dressed agency.

“No tool can rescue an offer nobody understands.”

The pattern is consistent. Visitors land on a homepage that describes the company and never states the deal. Enquiries arrive and wait two days for a reply. Emails go out that ask for attention and offer nothing in return.

None of those problems are fixed by buying software. Each of them can be found in an afternoon, if you know where to look. That is the job of this paper: find the problem first, in plain English, then decide whether a tool is needed at all.

B1

Dear Velquiry

Real questions from business owners, answered without jargon. Names withheld.

Dear Velquiry, my agency says our ads are “performing well.” My phone has not rung more than usual in four months. Who do I believe?

— Puzzled in Plano

DEAR PUZZLED: Believe the phone. Ask the agency for one number: how many paying customers came from the ads last month, and what each one cost. If they can’t tell you, they are reporting on the ads, not on your business.

Dear Velquiry, my nephew says I need an AI chatbot on my website. I sell kitchen remodels. Does he have a point?

— Skeptical in Spokane

DEAR SKEPTICAL: Maybe later. First check what happens when a real person fills in your form today. If a human replies within the hour, a chatbot adds little. If nobody replies for two days, fix that before you automate anything.

Dear Velquiry, we have 9,000 people on our email list and nobody opens anything. Should we buy a better email tool?

— Unopened in Ohio

DEAR UNOPENED: Your tool is fine. Read your last three subject lines out loud. If none of them promise the reader something useful, that’s the fix. Give before you ask.
